## The test I applied

The rework says two things: the advocate once tested his claim before filing because something made him; today almost nothing does. A quotation earns a place in the body only if it does one of three jobs for that thesis: (a) shows *why the test must come before filing*, (b) shows *what testing consists of* (predicting the tribunal, which means speaking its language), or (c) shows *an institution that made the advocate do it*, or the moment such an institution was dismantled. Quotations about paradigms and vocabularies in general (Kuhn, Rorty, Feyerabend, Fish, Gadamer) do real work in the footnotes but not in the body: Scalia does not quote Rorty in the text, and neither should a note written in his register. The forum-choice material (Bourdieu, Klarman on control) stays in Section VI as history.

## Tier 1: put these in the body

**1. Fuller & Randall, Joint Conference Report, reprinted in Fuller, *Forms and Limits*, 92 Harv. L. Rev. at 383–84 [V].**
> "what starts as a preliminary diagnosis designed to direct the inquiry tends, quickly and imperceptibly, to become a fixed conclusion, as all that confirms the diagnosis makes a strong imprint on the mind, while all that runs counter to it is received with diverted attention." (383)
> "He plays his role badly, and trespasses against the obligations of professional responsibility, when his desire to win leads him to muddy the headwaters of decision" (384)

Where: I.B.1 beside Sharswood (P0121–P0123), and again at VI.B (P0282). Why first: it is the profession's own charter for partisanship conceding that the mind needs an adversary to avoid premature closure. The note's answer writes itself: the adversary system supplies the adversary only after filing, when the diagnosis has hardened; the Deliberative Body must supply him before. Lead-in: *The bar's own defense of the adversary system rests on a concession it has never followed to its conclusion.*

**2. Tetlock, 46 Soc. Psychol. Q. at 290–91 [V], with Lerner & Tetlock, 125 Psychol. Bull. at 257, 259 [V].**
> accountability is "much more effective in preventing than in reversing the biased assimilation process" (Tetlock 1983 at 291)

Where: the Rule 11 paragraph (P0306), directly after "Rule 11 is a post-filing cost and not … a pre-filing one." Why: it is the psychological reason the note's timing argument is right. Pre-decisional accountability produces self-criticism; post-commitment accountability produces defensive bolstering. That is the QCM against Rule 11 in one experimental finding. Lead-in: *The psychologists have measured the difference, and it is the difference between before and after.*

**3. Mercier & Sperber, 34 Behav. & Brain Sci. at 66 [V] (00e corrects NotebookLM's 65 to 66).**
> "It makes sense to look for arguments for our opinions before we find ourselves called upon to state them. If the search for arguments is successful, we will be ready. If not, then perhaps it might be better to adopt a weaker position, one that is easier to defend."

Where: introduction ¶2 (P0095), after the three outcomes of testing. Why: it is the historical disputant's three outcomes stated by evolutionary psychology, which is the kind of convergence the note's "Why do I turn to history" paragraph (P0113) promises. Lead-in: *The evolutionary psychologists have arrived, by another road, at the same three outcomes.*

**4. Cicero, *De Oratore* II.102–03 (Sutton & Rackham, Loeb) at 275 [V-OCR].**
> "in my own person and with perfect impartiality I play three characters, myself, my opponent and the arbitrator."

Where: Section V, Rome, beside the calumny oath (P0234), and again in VII.C beside the premortem (P0372). Why: the practice the oath compelled, described by the advocate who practiced it, two thousand years before the intelligence community named it. Use the Loeb wording, not the Watson translation NotebookLM quotes. Lead-in: *Cicero described the exercise before anyone thought to give it a name.*

**5. Kronman, *The Lost Lawyer* 149 [V].**
> "To be successful in any situation, an advocate must understand the concerns of the person he is addressing and modify his arguments accordingly. It follows that a lawyer arguing before a judge who is responsible for maintaining the well-being of the law must himself become a master of analysis from the judicial point of view, for otherwise he will not know what to say to advance his client's cause."

Where: the Skrmetti close (P0161), before "No informal forum could have given the Skrmetti Plaintiffs full relief," and again at VII.A (P0337). Why: it says what testing a claim consists of. To predict the Court you must think as the Court thinks, and a team that speaks one idiom cannot. Pair with Posner, *Reflections on Judging* 357 [V]: "I don't understand their inability to see their cases from the judges' perspective." Lead-in: *Testing a claim means predicting a tribunal, and that is not a passive art.*

**6. The Inns of Court readings and moots [V].**
> Middle Temple description (1539), 105 Selden Soc. lxx: the utter barristers "by all ways of learning and reason that can be invented do impugn his opinion … and at last the reader doth confute all their sayings and confirmeth his opinion"
> Denton, Bacon & Cary report (1540), 105 Selden Soc. lxi: "the youngest of continuance argueth first"
> Baker, 105 Selden Soc. lxxii–lxxiii: benchers could "argue against their own minds for the sake of argument"

Where: V.C, after the Wynflæd and Shillingford material (P0248), and VII.B–C (junior first, P0359–P0367). Why: the common-law bar's own formation was a forge, junior first, with the reader answering last. It gives Section V an advocacy institution (it now has only forums) and gives the note's junior-first prescription a six-century pedigree in the tradition we inherited. Lead-in: *The English bar did not leave the test to conscience. It built it into the calendar.*

**7. Baker, 105 Selden Soc. lxxv–lxxvi [V]: in 1778 the Inner Temple's "age-old obligation to moot was commuted to a cash payment," leaving "perfunctory ceremonies of no educational significance."**

Where: the Third Development, monetization (P0300–P0301). Why: the English forge was monetized away a century and a half before hourly billing did the same here. It is the one historical parallel the third development lacks. Lead-in: *The English got there first.*

**8. Mill, *On Liberty* ch. II [V-epub; print page needed].**
> "they have never thrown themselves into the mental position of those who think differently from them, and considered what such persons may have to say; and consequently they do not, in any proper sense of the word, know the doctrine which they themselves profess."
> and the sentence 00e records that a person who hears the other side only from his own teachers "is under no compulsion to hear both sides … the weakest part of what everybody says in defence of his opinion, is what he intends as a reply to antagonists."

Where: VII intro (P0327–P0328), where the note argues that an assigned devil's advocate is weaker than authentic dissent. Mill's insistence that the objections be heard "from persons who actually believe them" is that argument, in 1859, derived from Cicero's forensic practice. It is the bridge from Part V to Part VII. Lead-in: *Mill made the point, and he made it by pointing at Cicero.*

**9. Douglas, *How Institutions Think* 111 [V].**
> "The individual tends to leave the important decisions to his institutions while busying himself with tactics and details."

Where: introduction ¶1 (P0094), after "Choosing among these parallel forums is now the first step in the dispute-resolution process." Why: the forum-not-the-claim pathology in one sentence, from an anthropologist who was not writing about lawyers. Also the right replacement for the misplaced fn 37 (with Douglas 12–13). Lead-in: *Mary Douglas was not writing about lawyers, but she might as well have been.*

**10. Cardozo, *Nature of the Judicial Process* 174–75 [V].**
> "The spirit of the age, as it is revealed to each of us, is too often only the spirit of the group in which the accidents of birth or education or occupation or fellowship have given us a place."

Where: Section III opener (P0174), before Janis. Why: groupthink named by a judge in 1921, half a century before the psychologists. It lets the section open with a lawyer's authority rather than a psychologist's. Lead-in: *Cardozo saw it before Janis had a word for it.*

**11. Tushnet, *The NAACP's Legal Strategy Against Segregated Education, 1925–1950* (1987) [V]** (a different book from the Tushnet you cite): national-office review of "all steps before they are taken" and of "proposed pleadings" (68–69); Marshall, "you don't run into court with a legal case overnight" (105); "the naacp lost every graduate school lawsuit that it handled" where the national staff "could not control enthusiastic local attorneys" (53).

**12. Strebeigh, *Equal* 62 [V] (00e corrects the pin from 61–62).**

**13. The QCM that survived: Lazarus, 96 Geo. L.J. at 1496 and 1518 [V]; McGuire, *The Supreme Court Bar* 177–78 [V].**
> Lazarus 1518: the Supreme Court bar "may be serving a useful screening function"; counsel "advise clients against filing petitions, although that eliminates a business opportunity"

## One structural caution

Your research chat frames a fourth idea, that advocates fail "because they only speak one interpretive language." The tracked draft does not make that a fourth historical development, and I would not. It is the account of *what testing consists of* (Kronman, Posner, Lazarus), and it belongs where the note explains how a team tests a claim: the Skrmetti close and VII.A. Making it a cause alongside Sharswood, capacity, and billing would reopen the structure you just settled.
