## The test I applied

The rework says two things: the advocate once tested his claim before filing because something made him; today almost nothing does. A quotation earns a place in the body only if it does one of three jobs for that thesis: (a) shows *why the test must come before filing*, (b) shows *what testing consists of* (predicting the tribunal, which means speaking its language), or (c) shows *an institution that made the advocate do it*, or the moment such an institution was dismantled. Quotations about paradigms and vocabularies in general (Kuhn, Rorty, Feyerabend, Fish, Gadamer) do real work in the footnotes but not in the body: Scalia does not quote Rorty in the text, and neither should a note written in his register. The forum-choice material (Bourdieu, Klarman on control) stays in Section VI as history.

## Tier 1: put these in the body

**1. Fuller & Randall, Joint Conference Report, reprinted in Fuller, *Forms and Limits*, 92 Harv. L. Rev. at 383–84 [V].**
> "what starts as a preliminary diagnosis designed to direct the inquiry tends, quickly and imperceptibly, to become a fixed conclusion, as all that confirms the diagnosis makes a strong imprint on the mind, while all that runs counter to it is received with diverted attention." (383)
> "He plays his role badly, and trespasses against the obligations of professional responsibility, when his desire to win leads him to muddy the headwaters of decision" (384)

Where: I.B.1 beside Sharswood (P0121–P0123), and again at VI.B (P0282). Why first: it is the profession's own charter for partisanship conceding that the mind needs an adversary to avoid premature closure. The note's answer writes itself: the adversary system supplies the adversary only after filing, when the diagnosis has hardened; the Deliberative Body must supply him before. Lead-in: *The bar's own defense of the adversary system rests on a concession it has never followed to its conclusion.*

**2. Tetlock, 46 Soc. Psychol. Q. at 290–91 [V], with Lerner & Tetlock, 125 Psychol. Bull. at 257, 259 [V].**
> accountability is "much more effective in preventing than in reversing the biased assimilation process" (Tetlock 1983 at 291)

Where: the Rule 11 paragraph (P0306), directly after "Rule 11 is a post-filing cost and not … a pre-filing one." Why: it is the psychological reason the note's timing argument is right. Pre-decisional accountability produces self-criticism; post-commitment accountability produces defensive bolstering. That is the QCM against Rule 11 in one experimental finding. Lead-in: *The psychologists have measured the difference, and it is the difference between before and after.*

**3. Mercier & Sperber, 34 Behav. & Brain Sci. at 66 [V] (00e corrects NotebookLM's 65 to 66).**

**4. Cicero, *De Oratore* II.102–03 (Sutton & Rackham, Loeb) at 275 [V-OCR].**

**5. Kronman, *The Lost Lawyer* 149 [V].**
> "To be successful in any situation, an advocate must understand the concerns of the person he is addressing and modify his arguments accordingly. It follows that a lawyer arguing before a judge who is responsible for maintaining the well-being of the law must himself become a master of analysis from the judicial point of view, for otherwise he will not know what to say to advance his client's cause."

Where: the Skrmetti close (P0161), before "No informal forum could have given the Skrmetti Plaintiffs full relief," and again at VII.A (P0337). Why: it says what testing a claim consists of. To predict the Court you must think as the Court thinks, and a team that speaks one idiom cannot. Pair with Posner, *Reflections on Judging* 357 [V]: "I don't understand their inability to see their cases from the judges' perspective." Lead-in: *Testing a claim means predicting a tribunal, and that is not a passive art.*

**8. Mill, *On Liberty* ch. II [V-epub; print page needed].**

## One structural caution

Your research chat frames a fourth idea, that advocates fail "because they only speak one interpretive language." The tracked draft does not make that a fourth historical development, and I would not. It is the account of *what testing consists of* (Kronman, Posner, Lazarus), and it belongs where the note explains how a team tests a claim: the Skrmetti close and VII.A. Making it a cause alongside Sharswood, capacity, and billing would reopen the structure you just settled.
